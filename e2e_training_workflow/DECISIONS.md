# Design decisions and the evidence for each

Every framework-level choice in this workflow, what it replaced, and the measurement that forced it.
Nothing here is reasoning from first principles alone: each row names a number and where it came
from. Measurements are on 8×MI308X (gfx942), `rocm/primus:v26.4`, bare Megatron `pretrain_gpt.py
--sft`, Qwen2.5 0.5B/2GPU and Qwen2.5-Math 7B/8GPU, seq 4096.

`PLAN.md` carries the narrative; this file is the index. Raw artifacts live in
`/home/treemann/geak_train/runs/` (probe sidecars per rank, one directory per run).

## 1. The measurement contract

| # | Decision | What it replaced | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Headline is **useful** (non-pad, loss-unmasked) tokens/s/GPU, read from `loss_mask.sum()` | the stack's nominal `seq_len × batch / time / world` | **71.6%** of tokens are padding: nominal 16748 vs useful 4760 tok/s/GPU, a **3.5×** overstatement. Upstream Megatron reports no tokens/s at all. |
| 1.2 | Warmup end is **detected from step-time stability**, not a fixed skip count | `log_avg_skip_iterations`-style constants | Same 7B run: skip-2 → spread **12.75%**; skip to the measured end (~10) → **0.18%**. Median unchanged (11648.9 → 11647.5). **70× difference in gate sensitivity.** Warmup is also not constant: 10-11 steps at 7B, 1 at 0.5B with warm caches. |
| 1.3 | Report `spread` **and** `iqr_spread` **and** `outlier_steps` — never one number | a single dispersion figure | One clean 25-step run: raw **38.6%**, IQR **0.072%** — 22 of 24 steps inside 0.07%, two isolated stalls carry the rest. **500×.** Raw as a band gates nothing; hiding the stalls discards a real host-side signal. |
| 1.4 | Legs are compared on a **common window** (`compare_legs.py`), never as a ratio of two summaries | each leg on its own window | `useful_tokens` swings **±25%** step to step (7389..11945). Three runs of one config: own windows **863.2/862.6/872.2** (spread 1.1%), common window **872.4/871.9/872.2** (spread 0.06%). On one real pair: unaligned **+0.3294%** — a *win* against the band — vs aligned **+0.0235%**; **93% was window placement.** Data order is deterministic, so alignment cancels the term exactly. |
| 1.5 | A leg with **< 12 steady steps** is refused (`dispersion_reliable`) | using whatever IQR came out | `--iters 8` leaves 7 steady steps and the quartiles straddle the near-warmup value: IQR **1.243%** vs **0.078%** on a 30-step run of the same workload. Small *n* errs both ways; an artificially tight band is the dangerous one. |
| 1.6 | The band floor is **regime-dependent**: aligned **0.15%**, unaligned **0.5%** | one 0.3% floor | Six pairwise comparisons of one 7B config across four days: aligned worst \|Δ\| **0.0589%**, unaligned worst **0.4456%** (artifact up to 0.3867%). A single 0.3% floor is ~5× too loose for aligned *and* too tight for unaligned — 0.4456% > 0.3% means it manufactures wins. |
| 1.6b | The dispersion term is the **sampling error of a median**, `0.929 × iqr / √n`, and the floor is **per workload** (`ACROSS_JOB_FLOOR_PCT` in `workloads/*.env`) | `max(floor, max_iqr × 2)` | **Correction made 2026-09-23**, measured over 47 same-identity baseline pairs (true delta zero). The old formula was not merely conservative, it was **backwards**: 7B (worst \|aligned Δ\| **0.0823%** over 10 pairs) was assigned a **0.914%** band — 11× too loose — while 0.5B (worst **0.4966%** over 37 pairs) sat at a 0.15% floor and produced **6 false wins**. The widest band went to the most reproducible workload, because `worst_iqr_spread_pct` is a *within-leg* statistic: pearson vs \|aligned Δ\| is **+0.07** at 0.5B and **−0.20** at 7B, and the measured slope where it predicts at all is **0.147**, not 2.0. The term also omitted the 1/√n, pricing one step's spread as the uncertainty of a median of n — one error producing both failure directions. New: `max(FLOOR_WORKLOAD, 4·se_pair, 2·R_cal)` → **0/47 false wins** vs 6/47. Note the floor does the work: the dimensional fix *alone* is **worse** (15/47). Cheapest lever is neither: at 0.5B both-legs-≥29-steady-steps gives worst 0.2544% against 0.4966% when either leg had 19. |
| 1.4b | The **combined** figure is MEASURED, never derived from the per-candidate deltas | adding or compounding the accepted deltas | Measured 2026-09-29 on the two accepted kernels: **+12.36%** over four ref/cand pairings (spread 0.12 points), null control +0.0706%, and a window-immune `step_median_ms` cross-check at +12.324%. Adding predicts +12.00% (off by 0.36), compounding +12.34% (off by 0.013) — so **these two compound**, i.e. they shorten disjoint parts of the step. That is a fact about this pair, not a rule: two kernels contending for the same stretch of the critical path would sub-add. The same four legs give +7.39% to +12.95% — a **5.5-point spread** — if the windows are not aligned. |
| 1.7 | Reproducibility is **measured per workload**, the floor is only a floor | a global constant | Same six-pair method: **0.06%** at 7B, **0.31-0.42%** at 0.5B. Consistent with the step decomposition — idle is **2.35%** at 7B and **14.17%** at 0.5B, and host jitter does not cancel. |
| 1.8 | A **profiled** or **captured** run can never be a gate leg (`status: unusable`) | treating them as ordinary runs | Capture writes tens of MB from inside a backward hook: **4043.9** vs ~4755 useful tok/s/GPU on the same config, a **15%** dent. A profiler window perturbs the steps it traces by construction. |
| 1.9 | Occupancy sampled around every leg; contamination → **bounded re-measure**, then stop | assuming the box is ours | The node carries ~20 unrelated containers. Policy is re-measure (default 2 retries): re-measuring costs one leg, killing a neighbour could cost days of someone else's work, and the workflow cannot tell which it is. Exhausted retries are **fatal**, not a "no win". |
| 1.10 | `config_digest` covers **workload identity only**; `impl_digest` covers implementation; `digest_schema` versions the definition | one digest over the whole snapshot | Requiring the whole snapshot to match refused an attention-backend comparison outright, before looking at the trajectory. Separately, two runs of one workload disagreed only because the digest *definition* had changed — re-summarising from the sidecars gave identical digests, so that case is `stale_summary`, not "different workload". **Correction made 2026-09-22 (schema 3):** the split was right, the *coverage* was not. Identity held **five** realized fields, because the probe scraped nine `--flag value` pairs and nothing else. `attention_dropout` 0.0 vs 0.1 digested **identically** (`288f95e335ac66a4`) while step-1 loss moved 3.1689 → 4.3728 on identical data and throughput by +7.3%; the model architecture was never covered at all (a 0.5B and a 7B leg at equal mbs/gbs/seq would have matched); TP/PP/CP were listed but never captured, so the "parallel mapping" this row claimed was covered on **no leg ever measured**. Now 37 fields / 33 identity, versioned in the **snapshot** (`snapshot_keys: v3`) not the summariser — a v2 sidecar cannot be re-summarised into v3, so that pair must be **re-measured**. `impl_digest` had the mirror defect: it hashed the overlay manifest **basename** (blind to a swapped kernel under a kept filename) and now hashes file **contents**. |

## 2. The bottleneck model

| # | Decision | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Rank by **critical-path share**, not `pct_gpu_time`; a communication kernel is scaled by its **exposed** fraction | A fully overlapped collective can own 30% of GPU time and be worth zero. The inference layer's `pct_gpu_time × achievable_speedup` would send the budget there. |
| 2.2 | **Profile at the real scale.** A toy profile routes the budget to the wrong kernel | Head op changes *class*: 0.5B → attention backward 21.5%; 7B → GEMM `_Linear [[3584,3584]]` 19.0%, with `aten::mm` vocab-projection backward 18.6% second and attention third at 14.7%. Buckets also move: compute 82.8% → 96.08%, idle 14.17% → 2.35%. |
| 2.3 | Profiling is **probe-driven**, not a stack feature | `grep -c profile megatron/training/arguments.py` → **0**. Upstream ships no profiler arguments; the `--profile` family in Primus recipes is Primus's own. The capability manifest was corrected to say so. |
| 2.4 | A **read-only** config diagnosis substitutes for ConfigSweep in `fast`, with **two categories**: `blocking` (the win cannot appear) and `opportunity` (a bigger lever exists, but kernel wins still pay) | Without it the run spends its budget on kernels whose wins cannot appear. **Correction made 2026-09-21:** padding was classified as blocking with the reason "a kernel win is diluted by the same factor" — that is arithmetically false. The headline is `useful_tokens/step_time`; a kernel win shortens `step_time` and leaves `useful_tokens` alone, so a 20% win on a 19% head is **+3.95% at 70% padding and at 0% padding alike**. On the real 7B profile the verdict therefore flips from `config_first` to **`kernel_work_can_pay`** — the old rule would have told the first run to give up on work that would have paid. |
| 2.5 | Multi-rank profiling; skew reported, never averaged | Rank 0 vs rank 4 at 7B agree to 0.4 points; `rank_skew_pct` 0.001. The straggler bucket is empty at TP1×DP8 — worth re-checking at TP>1. |

## 3. The correctness gates

| # | Decision | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | L0 judges a candidate against **the error the original already carries**, not a chosen rtol | The untouched original scores `max_rel_err` **11.7** against its own fp32 reference, because `dgrad` is ~77% zeros (padding carries no gradient). Any fixed rtol is either blind or permanently red. |
| 3.2 | L0 checks **`scale_bias` = ⟨err,ref⟩/⟨ref,ref⟩**, not only magnitude and sign balance | A `×1.005` candidate passed **both** the magnitude check (the original's own bf16 error, 0.764, is *larger* than the bias) and the sign test (0.528). `sign_balance` detects an *additive* bias; a multiplicative one leaves it at 0.5. With `scale_bias`: **FAIL at +5.00e-03**, the injected value recovered exactly. |
| 3.3 | Gate on **all** outputs, not the forward | Sensitivity moves with scale: at 0.5B a `×1.005` bias is caught on `out`; at 7B a `×1.002` bias passes `out` on magnitude and is caught on `wgrad`/`dgrad` (+2.01e-03 / +2.00e-03). |
| 3.4 | L1 is a **step-indexed envelope** anchored on step 1, not a scalar over a window | Training is chaotic: two identical runs are bit-identical at step 1, 0.76% apart by step 5, **2.04 apart in absolute loss by step 21** (loss ≈ 3). Calibrating `max\|Δloss\|` over 25 steps yields a limit of **6.1 on a loss of 3** — a gate that passes anything. Step 1 is a pure forward on identical weights, so it is bit-reproducible (verified at 0.5B/2GPU *and* 7B/8GPU); `grad_norm` at step 1 is not (0.87% at 0.5B, 0.165% at 7B — more DP ranks average more of it away). **Correction made 2026-09-22:** anchoring on step 1 was right; the **envelope half did not discriminate at all.** Proven by null control — a same-kernel pair whose true delta is zero by construction — at both scales: at 0.5B the band failed *every* baseline-vs-baseline pair including its **own calibration pair**; at 7B the null control failed 8 loss / 10 grad_norm steps (worst 36.27×) against the candidate's 8 / 12 (worst 37.36×), i.e. **statistically indistinguishable**. Two causes: `envelope[i]` was `max−min` over the calibration legs, which with **two** legs is a single \|A−B\| sample that collapses to ~0 wherever those legs crossed; and the zero-width floor was `STEP1_REL_FLOOR_PCT` (0.05%) applied to *every* metric — a constant calibrated for a bit-reproducible step-1 loss, applied to a `grad_norm` measuring **152.10 / 218.77 / 83.44** across three identical runs. Fixed: `MIN_BAND_LEGS = 3` (5 recommended), per-metric `rel_floor_pct` measured from the calibration legs, and `calibrate()` now runs the null control **on itself** and stores `envelope_discriminating`. A band that fails its own control is **advisory** — reported, not voting — leaving the step-1 probe and the early-window sign balance as the blocking detectors. |
| 3.5 | The step-1 floor is **two-tier**: `overlay` 0.05%, `kernel_swap` 2.0% | Measured against an **L0-verified** equivalent pair (TE `_LayerNormLinear` vs a pure-PyTorch reimplementation, swapped in through the overlay): same kernels **0.000000%**, verified swap **1.748837%** — **35×** the original guess. The reference computes in fp32, so 1.75% is an *upper* bound for a same-precision swap. |
| 3.6 | `calibrate_swap` emits **`pair_usable`** from a 2×2 (two configs × two repeats) | Three "obvious" calibration pairs were rejected **by measurement**: `NVTE_USE_HIPBLASLT` on/off and `gradient_accumulation_fusion` on/off are **bit-identical** at step 1 (neither changes the kernels), and `--transformer-impl local` cannot run packed sequences at all. Two of them would have read as "kernel swaps are numerically free". |
| 3.6b | **L2 soak compares MEANS of replicate legs**, and every gate carries a **positive control** | one long candidate leg vs one long reference leg, judged against the reference spread | The v1 soak (5 legs × 300 steps) PASSED its candidate **and also passed a kernel with a deliberate ×1.004 gradient bias invoked 2400×/rank** — so the candidate's pass meant nothing. Measured: between-leg σ of terminal loss **0.01495**, bias effect **0.03742 = 2.50 σ**, v1's threshold 3 σ. Two "obvious" repairs were tested and fail: more REFERENCE legs cannot help (σ is a population spread, and a single candidate leg carries one full σ regardless, so the threshold tends to K·σ = 0.0448 > 0.0374), and averaging over more steps within a leg is *worse* (288-step mean offset 1.12 σ vs the 28-step tail's 1.29 σ — chaotic divergence is serially correlated, so there is no √n inside a run). Power comes only from replicate CANDIDATE legs: n=3/m=4 gives t = 3.28 at α=0.05. Corollary for the whole layer: **a gate whose sensitivity has not been demonstrated on a known-bad input is not a gate**, and this one shipped named-but-unspecified for two runs. |
| 3.6c | **Match the gate to the defect class.** Op-local numerics is L0's job; the trajectory gates are for what L0 structurally cannot see | assuming a longer, more end-to-end test is a stricter test | Measured on one defect, a ×1.004 gradient bias: **L0 rejects it in 11.5 s end to end** (~40 ms for the comparison itself) (`dgrad scale_bias +3.80e-03`, `wgrad +4.41e-03`, tolerance 2e-3), while the L1 envelope, both step-1 probes, a 5-leg soak (~5 h) and 6 further legs (~6 h) **all miss it entirely**. L0 evaluates a frozen microbatch in isolation, so an op-local bias is exactly what it resolves; the soak measures a chaotic system where that same bias shows up as **dispersion, not location** (positive-control sd 2.75× the reference's, variance ratio 7.6, while the means coincide). Consequence: a soak whose positive control is an op-local numerics defect will keep failing its control while the system is in fact well defended. A soak is for cross-step state, accumulation over thousands of invocations, allocator effects — and it may not be run again until a positive control of THAT class exists, because ~10 h that cannot demonstrate its own sensitivity buys nothing. |
| 3.6d | **The L2 soak's positive control must be a defect L0 cannot see BY CONSTRUCTION, and the soak's power must be computed before the GPU time is spent** | picking a plausible known-bad kernel and a threshold, then running | v1 and v2 both failed their control and the reason turned out to be two separate errors, not one. (a) **Wrong class**: a x1.004 gradient bias is op-local numerics, which L0 rejects in 11.5 s, so it never reached the soak. The v3 control is cross-step staleness -- blend the previous invocation's `gscale` into the current one, `g[n] + alpha*(g[n-1]-g[n])`. L0 evaluates ONE frozen microbatch in ONE call, so there is no previous invocation and a repeated identical input makes the blend bit-exactly the identity for **every** alpha: invisible by construction, not by luck. The lerp form is load-bearing -- written `(1-a)*cur + a*prev` the identity holds in algebra but leaves ~1 ulp in floating point, and the self-test caught my docstring overclaiming before it shipped. (b) **Underpowered**: with sigma = 0.01495, v1's 1 candidate leg against 4 reference legs has MDE = `t_crit*sigma*sqrt(1/n+1/m)` = **0.0532**, against a bias effect of 0.03742. v1 was short by 42% and **no threshold could have rescued it** -- the arithmetic takes a minute and was never done. v3 uses n=3 candidate vs m=3 reference (MDE 0.0339) and costs ~3 GPU-h rather than ~7, because the three 0923 reference legs are reusable: checked `snapshot_keys: v3`, overlay off, 300 steps, same `config_digest d1548a7a764bdada` -- verified rather than assumed, since reusing out-of-scope legs is an error already made once here. Criteria pre-registered in `knowledge/gate_convergence.md` before any v3 leg ran, including that the dispersion criterion is **weaker evidence by declaration** because it was motivated by watching v2's control. |
| 3.6e | **The x1.004 bias's "effect" of 0.03742 was ONE LEG, and its sign does not reproduce** | treating a single-leg delta as an effect size, and building two soak generations around it | Re-ran the analyzer over every soak leg on disk. v1's positive group (n=1) sits at **-0.03742 (2.50 sigma) -- the bad leg trained LOWER than the references**; v2's positive group (n=3) sits at **+0.01570 (1.05 sigma), Welch t = 0.659 against crit 3.180**. The sign flips, and -0.03742 is precisely what one draw from a sigma = 0.01495 population looks like. So the bias has **no demonstrated location effect on terminal loss**, and the number two soaks were designed around was noise. This STRENGTHENS 3.6c: the soak was not just underpowered, there was most likely nothing in location to find, because the defect is op-local and L0 resolves it in 11.5 s. Consequence for v3: the stale control's effect must be MEASURED before it is relied on; if it also shows no location effect, that is a finding about the defect class, not a licence to loosen a threshold. Two analyzer defects found while establishing this, both of which manufacture confident numbers from absent data: with one positive leg it crashed on `r[3]` after printing a delta, and pointed at a truncated leg it reported terminal loss **0.00000 for every leg** and MDE 0.00000 before dying later. It now refuses both, and prints each design's **MDE before the results** so an underpowered run is visible without reading the verdict backwards. |
| 3.6f | **The L2 soak is RETIRED**: between L0 and L1 there is no band of defects 300 steps and a handful of legs can resolve | repairing the soak by fixing its control, which is what v3 set out to do | v3 built a control L0 cannot see **by construction** -- cross-step staleness, bit-identical L0 output at alpha = 1.0 at the same line where a x1.004 bias is rejected -- and ran it exactly as pre-registered: 4 positive legs vs 4 clean overlay-matched legs, engagement verified at **19 200 fwd + 19 200 bwd per leg**. Result: null control **silent**, positive control **NOT REJECTED** (Welch t = 0.375 vs crit 2.780, delta +0.00576 = 0.63 sigma), dispersion F = 10.25 vs crit 15.44. Positive terminal losses 1.4226 / 1.4172 / **1.4777** / 1.4179 -- **dispersion, not location, for the third control running**. The token-aligned trajectory comparator fails the same control on the same legs (positive medians all inside the null range; one CLEAN pair separates further than any defective leg), so it is not promoted either. **The decision is arithmetic, not preference**: at alpha = 1.0 L1's sign test rejects this defect for FREE in a leg that was running anyway; at alpha = 0.25 where L1 is silent, closing the gap from delta 0.00576 to MDE 0.01587 needs (0.01587/0.00576)^2 ~ 7.6x more legs, n = m ~ 30, about **30 GPU-hours**, for a class L1 catches one notch up in severity; and the op-local class is L0's at 11.5 s. Three control generations, ~17 GPU-hours, zero rejections. The disposition was committed to IN WRITING before the data, which is the only reason it can be acted on now without arguing. The control is not wasted -- it becomes **L1's** positive control, the first one L1 has had of a class L0 cannot see. |
| 3.4b | The L1 **per-step envelope is retired from voting** (2026-09-28); L1 = step-1 probe + early-window sign balance | patching the envelope a fifth time | It fired in all four real runs and was load-bearing in none: each firing was falsified by a control, most sharply when a **true-zero-delta pair was rejected 2.5× harder** than the candidate (worst 17.928 vs 7.107). Its ceiling is structural — measured by injection, a **+25% loss shift over the whole steady window is invisible to it**, because by step 12 three identical legs already disagree by ~8.9% and it allows safety × that. Four patches (≥3 legs, p10 floor, out-of-sample holdout, argv/length scope) each fixed a real defect and none produced a usable verdict. Still computed and reported; simply carries no verdict. |
| 3.4c | Every trajectory detector must be held to a **measured clean-pair range**, not an assumed one | `sign_balance in {0,1}` as proof of bias | Measured 2026-09-28, in one run: a pair with a **true delta of exactly zero** scored loss sign balance **0.000** while another scored 0.750. The old rule would have rejected a leg for being identical to itself — and it DID reject `attn_fmha_bwd_retry` as the sole firing detector, a candidate L0 rates **strictly more accurate than the incumbent** (which fails an analytic `dq` invariant by **1355.79×**). With `EARLY_STEPS=8` the samples are serially correlated, so they are nothing like 8 coin flips. `calibrate()` now records the range its own clean pairs produce and the detector fires only outside it. **Open, and not a tuning problem:** a sign test cannot separate *a correctness fix that descends faster* from *a bias pointing downhill* — a candidate that fixes a bug looks identical to one that introduces one. |
| 3.4d | **The L0/L1 rule**: when L0 rates a candidate strictly more accurate than the incumbent on all outputs, a one-sided move toward LOWER loss is corroboration and the sign test may not reject on it alone | letting the verdict depend on which layer is read last | `attn_fmha_bwd_retry` was rejected on sign balance 0.000 — the only detector that fired — while L0 rated it strictly more accurate, the incumbent failing an analytic `dq` invariant by **1355.79×**. Both true, no rule. The **asymmetry is the safety argument**: a one-sided move toward HIGHER loss still blocks even with that L0 verdict, and either direction blocks without it — a *less* accurate op descending faster is exactly what a downhill bias looks like. Waivers are advisories, never silent; all four combinations pinned by tests. Honest scope: the earlier clean-range fix already covered this candidate (clean pairs reach 0.0 on both synthetic and real data), so this rule is a second, narrower reason and is not credited with resolving the case that motivated it. |
| 3.4e | **The L1 sign test is held to a STRICT one-sided rule with measured rates, not the band's 3-pair range** | min..max of the band's three clean pairs as the null range | Over **693 clean same-identity v3 pairs** a value outside that range is what noise does most of the time: **loss 63.5%, grad_norm 55.6% false reject** (at n=8 a fair coin lands in {0.5,0.625} only 49% of the time; three draws cannot bound it). The strict rule -- every one of >=5 non-identical early diffs the same sign -- measures **5.1% / 0.6%** on the same pairs and fires on a real gross backward defect (7B attention, step-1 grad_norm 74 -> 8.5, dropout-0 pair: 0.0 / 0.0). Retiring the test outright was tried and reverted: a +0.9 loss shift from step 3, step 1 untouched, would then pass L1 entirely. Blind spots stated: staleness, the x1.004 bias, and at dropout 0.1 even the real defect (0.375/0.5) -- the step-1 probe caught that one. Also withdrawn: the claim that L1 caught alpha=1.0 staleness (one 30-step leg, overlay-confounded reference; 0.375-0.75 against matched references), and my soak floor 'between alpha 0.25 and 1.0' (a second alpha=1.0 leg sat at -0.20 sd where the first was +6.25 sd). Verdicts that rested on the old rule -- the 0.5B attention rejection, `attn_fmha_bwd_retry` -- have no sound basis on that evidence. |
| 3.4f | **L1 gains a step-1 GRADIENT probe, and config levers must pass L1 to be accepted** | a forward-only step-1 probe, and a ConfigSweep that called `gateVerdict` with no l1 at all | Found by the packing equivalence probe: Megatron's **flash** attention backend on this ROCm stack has a forward within 0.11% of fused (dropout 0) and a **gradient 2.7x too small** -- step-1 grad_norm 17.45 vs fused 47.93, with fused matching HF's 47.89 to 0.08%; in the production recipe 26.7 vs 68.0. The roadmap had called fused/flash 'a proven-comparable pair' on a 0.2917% step-1 LOSS delta: the forward, never the backward. Step-1 grad_norm is held to both controls: **null** -- 696 clean same-identity pairs, |rel diff| p50 0.26%, p99 1.05%, **max 1.40%**; **positive** -- flash -61%/-64%, the rejected 7B attention candidate -88.5% (caught twice). Limit **10%**: 7x the worst null, 6x below the mildest defect; the three accepted kernel stacks sit at 3.9-4.2% (they move step-1 gradients a real ~4%, and pass). Separately: **ConfigSweep never ran L1** -- a Class-A lever was judged on throughput alone, the very objection used to refuse Class B, and flash would have been accepted the moment it measured faster. The Config Tuner now returns `trajectory_gate.py compare` verbatim as `l1`; without it a win becomes `no_correctness_gate`. The dry run proves both refusals and was confirmed to FAIL with the fix removed. |
| 3.4g | **CORRECTION to 3.4f: step-1 grad_norm cannot compare implementations finely; limit 30%, not 10%** | calibrating an implementation-change detector on same-implementation pairs | The same isolated computation through two attention implementations that are both correct at op level reads total grad_norm 48.01 (sdpa) vs **54.06** (FA2), 12.6% apart; `embed_tokens` carries ~99% of the norm, and even non-embedding norms differ 4-8%. The 696-pair null (max 1.40%) was SAME-implementation and therefore the wrong null; 10% would have rejected a correct attention kernel. Recalibrated to **30%** against correct changes (<=12.6%) and defects (>=61%). The same evidence **withdraws** the 'packed backward 4.3% off' reading: it sits inside the correct-implementation spread, FA2 packed vs isolated is only -1.2%, and at kernel level `flash_attn_varlen_func` vs per-segment is bit-identical in out/dq/dk/dv. Flash's 2.7x stays a defect by this measure, root cause unlocalised (the varlen kernel itself is exact). |
| 3.4h | **The step-1 gradient probe is RETIRED from voting: its null failed on the packed reference workload** | the 30% limit of 3.4g | The first packed-stack kernel run rejected `mlp_fc1_gemm` (+1.52%; L0 rates it exactly as accurate as the incumbent) on step-1 grad_norm +64%. The Convergence Auditor rejected it and named the missing control: perturb the REFERENCE's fc1 forward by an equally accurate GEMM solution. Run with the candidate overlay, only the fwd solution changed: **81426 (bit-identical to the heuristic) 141.97; 90114 (1 ulp) 202.87 (+44%); 83027 (1 ulp, other kernel family) 227.51 (+62%); 90000 (the candidate) 228-231 (+64%)**, reference 139.9-141.8. Accuracy-neutral changes reach +64%, defects start at 61%: no threshold separates them, so it cannot vote. It stays as an advisory. ## 5. Orchestration

| # | Decision | Evidence / rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **Two** modes (`fast`, `deep`), not three | A training gate is two job launches and blocks every lane, so the useful distinction is "fixed config" vs "search the config too". |
| 5.2 | A gate takes the **whole box**: lanes drain first (`makeBarrier`) | A TP1×DP8 job occupies all 8 GPUs, so the inference layer's "lanes and the serving slot coexist" has no analogue. Reserving a subset fails on *measurement basis*, not performance: the parallel mapping is part of the workload identity, so a DP6 number is not about the DP8 configuration. |
| 5.3 | Budget counts **gates**, not only wall clock | One A/B ≈ two job launches (checkpoint load + warmup + N steps) and it blocks every lane: at 7B a leg is ~7 minutes. Gates are the scarce resource — the opposite of the inference layer. |
| 5.4 | Six **ConfigSweep seam hooks** exist in `fast` with trivial behaviour | Hooks 1-3 (carried config, config as an explicit argument, independently callable reprofile/restrategize) are the expensive ones to retrofit: threading a config through every measurement afterwards touches every call site, and that is where a silently stale reference leg would hide. |
| 5.5 | `deep_mode` **refuses to run**; a phase with no role **fails loudly** | Reporting a fast-mode result under a deep label, or silently skipping a gate, produces a result that looks complete and is not. |
| 5.6 | An accepted candidate becomes the **reference** for the next gate, and triggers a re-profile | Deltas compound on the carried stack, and an accept moves which kernels dominate. |
| 5.7 | Memory is a **currency, not a metric**; dormant in `fast` | It is only spendable through a config change (raise MBS, drop recompute), which `fast` does not have. Recorded as `banked_memory_pct`. Headroom measured: peak **78 GB of 192 GB** at 7B. |
| 5.8 | `bench_train.sh` works identically from **host or container** | The orchestrator runs wherever Claude Code runs; the stack only exists in the image. Relative paths are resolved before re-entry (the container starts in `/`), and a path outside every mount is rejected with the mounts listed. Both failure modes were hit in testing. |

## 6. Framework portability (built in v1, exercised in v2)

| # | Seam | Why it is not deferrable |
|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | Canonical config **IR** | Otherwise the KB fills with Megatron/Primus literals and the cross-run knowledge — the most valuable asset here — does not transfer. |
| 6.2 | **Self-evidencing measurement** (torch-level probe) | Justified as portability insurance; turned out to be *required* on framework #1, since upstream Megatron logs no tokens/s. A log parser would also cost one parser per stack and yield numbers that are not comparable across stacks. |
| 6.3 | **Capability manifest**, route on capability not framework name | Already caught one over-claim: `step_ranged_profiler` was declared true and corrected to false when upstream turned out to have no profiler arguments. |

## 7. Bare Megatron on ROCm — compatibility facts

| Symptom | Cause | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| `TypeError: NoneType + str` in `_compile_dependencies` | `cpp_extension.CUDA_HOME` is None on ROCm; the path builds `.cu` fused softmax | `rocm_compat.py` no-ops it, as Primus does |
| `expected 1D tensor` / `Boolean value of Tensor ... ambiguous` in both rope paths | upstream `--sft` THD packing returns a per-sample 1D `cu_seqlens`, the collate stacks it to `[MBS, n+1]`, and the rope helpers assume 1D | squeeze the vacuous batch dim at MBS=1; **refuse MBS>1 loudly** (merging rows changes semantics) |
| `NotImplementedError: only for SFTTokenizer` | `--tokenizer-type HuggingFaceTokenizer` | use `SFTTokenizer` |
| `NVTE_FLASH_ATTN set to 0, but expected 1` | image sets `NVTE_FUSED_ATTN=1`; `--attention-backend auto` disagrees | `--attention-backend fused` (now overridable — it is a config choice, not an adapter fact) |
| `int // NoneType` in `get_train_valid_test_num_samples` | `--eval-interval` is used unconditionally even with `--eval-iters 0` | set it |
| Prompt format | the three non-`default` formats hardcode Nemotron templates and pad tokens absent from Qwen's vocabulary | `default`; it skips prompt masking, which does not touch the compute graph — padding is still masked, so packing stays measurable |

## 8. Process lessons (they cost real runs)

* **Never edit a script while it is executing.** bash reads lazily by byte offset; an in-place edit shifts the remaining bytes and the running process parses garbage. It destroyed the summary stage of a completed 8-GPU run (the probe sidecars survived, so it was recoverable by re-summarising).
* **Verify that an edit applied.** A string-replace that silently does not match leaves every later edit keyed on its intended output no-op'ing too, while the tooling reports success — it only proves the script ran. **Four GPU runs** were lost to this before edits started asserting the pattern matched.
* **A negative test is worth more than a passing one.** The `×1.005` candidate passing every check is what produced `scale_bias`; the 2×2 control is what exposed three non-swaps as non-swaps.
* **Test the call site, not just the function.** `gateVerdict()` had correct aligned-delta arithmetic and a unit test proving it — but the test passed `comparison` straight in, while the one production call site never forwarded it. The aligned path was covered and *never executed*. Two full runs gated on raw ratios.
* **"Declared and never read" is a defect class, not an untidiness.** Eight `*_SCHEMA` constants were defined and never passed to `agent()`, so every role returned a string and a Setup phase that had genuinely measured two clean baselines was discarded as "produced no baseline". An audit for the same pattern then found four more: `VALIDATE_SCHEMA.delta_pct`, `STRATEGY_SCHEMA.stop` (the Architect could say "nothing here pays" and be overruled by nothing at all) and `FINALIZE_SCHEMA.engagement_recheck` (Finalize was *ordered* to re-check engagement; a failure was ignored). The agent does the work and the orchestrator drops it — and it looks exactly like success.
* **A syntax checker cannot see loader rules.** `node --check` passes a second top-level `export` (Node 22 auto-detects ESM, where it is valid) and passes `Date.now()` — but the Workflow loader strips only the leading `export const meta` and parses the rest as a *function body*, and the sandbox's clock bindings exist and **throw** when called. Both are now text-rule checks in `preflight.sh`, regression-tested against the exact historical failures.
* **A guard that cannot fail is worth nothing.** After adding those two preflight rules, each was re-run against the file that actually broke: each fires on its own defect and stays silent on the other's.
* **One probe dir is ONE leg, and nothing enforced it.** `train_probe` opened the sidecar `"a"` and `bench_train.sh` never cleared the directory, so re-using a `--tag` appended. `td_baseline_legA` holds a 0.5B run followed by a 7B run in the same `probe_rank0.jsonl`; `probe_summarize.load_rank()` took the **last** header but **all** steps and produced one "7B leg" at 777.1 tok/s with `worst_iqr_spread_pct` **91.69%** — which would assign a **183% band**, a gate that cannot reject anything. `ranks_agree_on_argv` could not catch it *because* last-header-wins made rank 0 agree with the uncorrupted ranks. Now refused at both ends, plus an absolute 25% IQR ceiling in `legUsable`. It never reached a headline (Stage 2 used a clean baseline and the Director re-measured independently) — a latent landmine, not a contamination.
* **Pre-register, then let the result stand.** The L2 criteria were written to `knowledge/gate_convergence.md` and committed before the first soak leg ran. Two of them then turned out to be defective — `sign_balance` was *vacuous* (its K=3 limit was 0.6146 while the statistic cannot deviate from 0.5 by more than 0.5, so no data could fail it) and drift-slope carried no signal (the positive control's slope was *smaller* than the candidate's). Both are recorded and withdrawn; **K was not retuned after seeing the data**, which is the whole point of writing it down first. The same discipline caught a wrong recommendation of my own: "add 5–9 reference legs" was published when v1 failed, and the arithmetic done before spending that GPU time showed it could not have worked.
* **Two instruments beat one.** Asking a second analyst to cross-check every throughput conclusion against `step_median_ms` — which cannot be moved by window composition — paid off exactly once, and that once was the corrupted leg: 51 of 55 pairs agreed to <0.01 points, and all 4 disagreements >0.1 involved `td_baseline_legA`. `step_median_ms` was right. The disagreement between instruments *was* the finding.

## 9. What the first two end-to-end runs found

Stage 1 (0.5B/2GPU, 30 agents, 2h39m) and Stage 2 (7B/8GPU, 35 agents, 4h04m). **Every launch-blocking
defect was plumbing; none was in the measurement science.** Each surfaced strictly later in the
pipeline than the last, which is the shape you want.

| # | Defect | Cost | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A second top-level `export const _test` | Failed at launch, 0 agents | Keep the binding, drop the keyword; preflight rule |
| 2 | `Date.now ? Date.now() : 0` — the binding **exists and throws**, so the feature-detect was always truthy | Failed at launch, 0 agents | `args.t0_epoch_ms` + a clock read through an agent (agents run bash); preflight rule |
| 3 | Eight `*_SCHEMA` declared, never passed to `agent()` | **Discarded a good baseline** after 7 min of clean GPU measurement | Wire `PHASE_SCHEMA`; `coerceResult()` as backstop |
| 4 | `training → kernel_workflow → kernel_lane` = 3 nesting levels; the runtime allows 1 | Threw **after** extraction, 26 min in | Call `kernel_lane.js` directly, as `e2e_workflow.js` already did |
| 5 | `integ.comparison` never forwarded to `gateVerdict()` | Both runs gated on raw ratios; a +7.85% "win" was really +4.54% | Forward it; a raw ratio may reject but never accept |
| 6 | L1 per-step envelope discriminates nothing | Both Directors had to hand-recalibrate mid-flight | Null control inside `calibrate()`; advisory demotion |
| 7 | `config_digest` covered 5 fields | Two different workloads shared one identity | Schema 3 |
| 8 | `impl_digest` hashed a path string | Cited as provenance it could not support | Hash contents |

**What worked on first execution:** the six role prompts (65 agents across both runs, zero errored or
returned empty); the whole measurement layer; `compare_legs.py`, which is the tool that *disproved*
the +7.85%; the L0 negative control (a ×1.004 bias was caught on `scale_bias` while its `dgrad`
magnitude was identical to the original's); the Architect's routing (`cp_share` 6.41% → predicted
3.94% → measured **+4.54%**); and the Director's independent arbitration, which overturned an
upstream conclusion in **both** runs and showed its working each time.

**The honest reading of Stage 2:** the accepted kernel is real (+4.54%, corroborated by
`step_median_ms` −4.348% ⇒ +4.5456%, a metric immune to window composition, against +0.053% on a null
pair), but the run's most valuable output was the eight defects above, not the speedup. L2 soak is
still **recommended and not run** for a `risk_class=backward` overlay, and the win is specific to this
data's ~70% padding — it shrinks toward zero on packed data.
